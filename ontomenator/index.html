Aphanipoiesis
the unseen bringing-forth

Nora Bateson (2021). Aphanis (αφανής — unseen, obscured) + poiesis (ποίησις — making, bringing forth). "An unseen coalescence toward vitality." The third in a trilogy of neologisms following Warm Data (2017) and Symmathesy (2016).

Living systems are constantly forming through mutual interaction at scales and in registers that resist observation. The change before the change — the composting underneath. Pathology and vitality may be describable once manifest, but the ways they come to occur are at least in part unseen.

Extends Gregory Bateson's work on abduction — the way one context describes another — into the zone where contextual mutual learning hasn't yet surfaced as identifiable pattern. "The tissues between the gaps hold the implicit premises of the system."

The productive tension with [ontomena]: if being and appearing are inseparable, what do we make of formative processes that are genuinely real but genuinely unseen? Resolution: "showing" is not limited to human perception. Being is always already in mutual manifestation — to itself, at every scale. Aphanipoiesis names this same process from the perspective of the human who can't yet see it.

You can't "do aphanipoiesis" — plunging your hand into the stream disrupts the stream. This echoes [Bergson's] critique of the spatialising intellect. The panonticist commitment: insist that the unseen is real while accepting that our relationship to it must be tending rather than grasping.

Sonification as aphanipoietic practice: not forcing the geomagnetic field to reveal itself, but widening the channel through which it was always showing itself — just not to human ears. See [Magnet Opus].

Introduction

The Ontomenator

a conceptual map

This is a spatial map of interlinked ideas, designed to be explored on a larger screen — the full experience lays the concepts out as a canvas you can roam.

On a phone, that spatial view is cumbersome, so this adapted version offers a straight reading route through the nodes instead. You can still open the map to see the whole shape.
