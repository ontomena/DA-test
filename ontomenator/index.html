politics / epistemology
Homophily
people like people like themselves

Homophily — from the Greek: homo (same) + philia (love). The tendency of individuals to associate and bond with similar others: same education, same backgrounds, same economic experience, same social networks. In political and economic discourse, homophily is the structural mechanism by which the establishment perpetuates its blindspots without conspiracy or malice. The cave seals itself.

Barry Ferns, "Barry's Economics" (2025): "Intelligence in a closed system isn't protection against bias — it's how bias becomes confident." Ferns' insight is the critical one: intelligence amplifies whatever the closed system already contains. The smarter the echo chamber, the more sophisticated its rationalisations for not hearing what's outside it. See [Confidence Amplification].

Rory Stewart's confidence in established thinking prevents him from taking millions of lived experiences seriously. This is not dishonesty — it is homophily at the epistemic-class level. Genuinely engaged, genuinely intelligent, genuinely trying to understand — but shaped by networks that systematically exclude most of the people whose lives he is discussing. See [The Rest is Politics], [Habitus].

The mechanism is structural not subjective. Homophily produces information cascades within closed networks — each member reinforces the others' certainties, the group's consensus hardens into common sense, and heterodox positions become literally unthinkable: not dismissed but unencountered. See [Wealth Pump].

Homophily is an-aisthesis at the structural level: the managed narrowing of what can be perceived, achieved not by propaganda but by the fact of who you know and what experiences they share. Widening aisthesis requires widening the social network through which experience arrives. See [Aisthesis], [Cave], [Real Economy].

A critical case study: Larry Fink, CEO of BlackRock (the world's largest asset manager, $12 trillion under management), as WEF interim co-chair, opened Davos 2026 by acknowledging the legitimacy crisis directly. "For many people, this meeting feels out of step with the moment: elites in an age of populism, an established institution in an era of deep institutional distrust. And there's truth in that critique." Then the extraordinary line: "But now for the harder question — will anyone outside this room care?" Asked from inside the room. To the people inside the room. By the person chairing the room.

Fink's economic confession: "Since the fall of the Berlin Wall, more wealth has been created than in any time prior in human history, but in advanced economies, that wealth has accrued to a far narrower share of people than any healthy society can ultimately sustain." Most of it, he acknowledged, to the kinds of people who attend Davos. This is the empirical claim of Stevenson, Zucman and Mamdani — confirmed by the person who runs the asset manager that owns much of it.

And yet: when asked about Carney's "rupture" framing, Fink declined to endorse it. He called the speech "for the ages" but refused to commit to its diagnosis. This is Confidence Amplification's limiting case — the intelligence of the closed system producing a critique sophisticated enough to discharge the pressure without committing to structural change. The cave dweller naming the cave, from inside it, to the other cave dwellers, while remaining in it. Naming the sign in the window is not the same as taking it down. See [Confidence Amplification], [Cave].

Aphanipoiesis
the unseen bringing-forth

Nora Bateson (2021). Aphanis (αφανής — unseen, obscured) + poiesis (ποίησις — making, bringing forth). "An unseen coalescence toward vitality." The third in a trilogy of neologisms following Warm Data (2017) and Symmathesy (2016).

Living systems are constantly forming through mutual interaction at scales and in registers that resist observation. The change before the change — the composting underneath. Pathology and vitality may be describable once manifest, but the ways they come to occur are at least in part unseen.

Extends Gregory Bateson's work on abduction — the way one context describes another — into the zone where contextual mutual learning hasn't yet surfaced as identifiable pattern. "The tissues between the gaps hold the implicit premises of the system."

The productive tension with [ontomena]: if being and appearing are inseparable, what do we make of formative processes that are genuinely real but genuinely unseen? Resolution: "showing" is not limited to human perception. Being is always already in mutual manifestation — to itself, at every scale. Aphanipoiesis names this same process from the perspective of the human who can't yet see it.

You can't "do aphanipoiesis" — plunging your hand into the stream disrupts the stream. This echoes [Bergson's] critique of the spatialising intellect. The panonticist commitment: insist that the unseen is real while accepting that our relationship to it must be tending rather than grasping.

Sonification as aphanipoietic practice: not forcing the geomagnetic field to reveal itself, but widening the channel through which it was always showing itself — just not to human ears. See [Magnet Opus].
